By Hannah Lowe

Demand for traditional office space in the city center has fallen for the third year in a row, according to a report released Monday by the Metropolitan Business Council. The vacancy rate for downtown offices now stands at 18 percent, up from 11 percent three years ago.

The report attributes the decline mainly to the growth of remote and hybrid work. Many companies have reduced the size of their offices, choosing instead to rent shared workspaces for days when employees come in.

However, not all landlords are struggling. Buildings that offer modern amenities, such as fitness centers, outdoor terraces, and flexible floor plans, have kept occupancy rates above 90 percent. "Tenants are willing to pay more for space that gives employees a reason to come to the office," said council director Martin Abe.

Some property owners are now converting older office buildings into apartments, a trend the council expects to continue.

Dalton Foods to Introduce Four-Day Workweek

CHESTER (July 8) — Dalton Foods, a maker of frozen meals, will test a four-day workweek at its Chester headquarters beginning September 1. Office employees will work ten hours a day, Monday through Thursday, instead of eight hours a day, five days a week. Factory employees are not included in the trial, which will last six months.

Company spokesperson Irene Faulk said the change is intended to improve employee satisfaction and reduce commuting costs. "If the trial succeeds, we will consider expanding it to our other offices," she said.
